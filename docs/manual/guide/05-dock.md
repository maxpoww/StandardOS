# Apps and the dock

The dock at the bottom holds the apps you use; the box it opens holds everything else — every app, apps to install, your files and the settings. Most of the time you will tap <kbd>Super</kbd>, type a few letters and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>.

## The dock

The dock stays visible while no window covers its place, and steps aside when one does; touch the bottom edge with the pointer to bring it back. It holds:

- **your pinned apps** — drag an app onto the dock to pin it, drag it off to unpin it;
- three fixed tiles: **Apps** (every app, as pages), the **Recycle Bin**, and the **Control panel** (Controls);
- after a line, **the windows that are open or minimized** — a minimized window shows its picture; click it to bring it back.

A new dock holds only the three fixed tiles: pin the apps you use.

| To… | Do this |
|---|---|
| Open an app, or go to its window | Click its tile |
| Open a new window of an app | <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+click, right-click or middle-click |
| Make a group of apps | In the Apps grid, drop one app on another; the group opens in a line under the row |
| Throw a file away | Drag it onto the Recycle Bin |
| Uninstall an app you installed | Drag it onto the Recycle Bin |
| Bring a window forward while dragging a file | Hold the file over that app's tile for a moment |

In **Controls → Launcher** you can move the dock to the left or the right, keep it away until you call it (**Stay away**), and choose how apps are arranged (by use, by name, or by hand). With the box open, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>+</kbd> and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>−</kbd> change the icons' size.

## The box

A tap of <kbd>Super</kbd> (press it and let go, alone), or scrolling up on the dock, opens the box; another tap, or <kbd>Esc</kbd>, closes it. It has three rows:

- **Apps** — every app on your computer.
- **Install** — apps, web apps and modules you can add. See [Installing apps](#guide-apps).
- **Files** — your files.

When you type, settings that match appear in a **System** row too.

### Searching

Just type. The search forgives typos and understands what apps do: *office* finds LibreOffice, *browser* finds the browser. For files it understands kinds and times:

| Type | Finds |
|---|---|
| `pdf`, `photos`, `videos`, `music`, `docs`, `folders`, `zips` | Files of that kind |
| `today`, `yesterday`, `this week`, `last week`, `this month`, `this year` | Files changed then |
| `>10mb`, `<500kb`, `big` | Files of that size |

Words work in Spanish too (*fotos*, *música*, *hoy*…). **Show all in Files** at the end of the list opens the search in Standard Files.

### Doing more with what you found

- **<kbd>Enter</kbd>** opens the selection (an app always opens in a new window from the box).
- **<kbd>Shift</kbd>** on a file shows what you can do with it: copy it, show it in Files, open it with another app, Quick Look, copy its path, move it to the Bin.
- **<kbd>Shift</kbd>** on an app shows its own row: its actions (for example *Open copied image* for GIMP, or a site's pages for a web app), its recent files, **Show all windows**, **Close all windows**, and **Uninstall** (click twice).
- After an app's name and a space, the search goes *inside* it: `amazon shoes` searches Amazon.
- **<kbd>Space</kbd>** on a file shows it big ([Quick Look](#guide-files--quick-look)); <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Enter</kbd> shows it in Standard Files.
- **<kbd>/</kbd>** in an empty search starts a deep file search, with your recent files, new files and places.
- **<kbd>.</kbd><kbd>/</kbd>** turns the box into a terminal — for experts.

## Two quick bubbles

- **<kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>J</kbd> — fast launch.** A small bubble in the middle of the screen that searches only apps. Type, <kbd>Enter</kbd>, done.
- **<kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>H</kbd> — quick controls.** The same bubble for settings: it opens on the three you use most; type a few letters and <kbd>Enter</kbd> opens that setting in a box.
