# Windows and desks

StandardOS places windows for you: each new window takes its share of the screen beside the others (*tiling*), so nothing is hidden behind anything. When you prefer windows you move and size yourself, they can *float* — one window, or all of them.

## Tiling and floating

- **Tiling** is how StandardOS starts. A new window opens beside the window under the pointer, and the windows of a desk share the screen. A desk with a single window uses the whole screen, with no border.
- **Floating** windows can be moved and resized freely, have rounded corners and a title bar, and come forward when you click them.

| To… | Do this |
|---|---|
| Float or tile one window | <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd>, or the window pill's float button |
| Make every window float | Gear → **Floating windows** |
| Keep a window its own size, in its place in the layout | <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>P</kbd> (pseudo-tile) |
| Make a window fill the screen | The window pill's fullscreen button |
| Keep a floating window on top, on every desk | The window pill's **pin** |
| Move a window | Drag its title bar, or <kbd>Super</kbd>+drag anywhere on it |
| Resize a window | Drag its edge, or <kbd>Super</kbd>+arrow keys |
| Close a window | <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>Q</kbd>, <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>F4</kbd>, or the window pill's × |

Clicking a window gives it the keyboard; moving the pointer over a window does not. A file you drag never raises the windows it passes over: hold it still over a window for a moment to bring that window forward.

## Title bars

Floating windows wear a title bar drawn by StandardOS. On its left, three coloured buttons: red **closes** the window, green puts it **back into the layout** (on a tiled window, it makes it float), and orange **minimizes** it to the dock. At the other end, the **pin** and the **card** button. Standard Files and web apps also get back and forward arrows when there is somewhere to go. The browser draws its own buttons instead.

In **Controls → Tiles** you can choose which windows wear a title bar (floating, tiled), put the buttons on the right, make them grey instead of coloured, and set the space between windows.

## Windows remember their place

When every window floats (gear → **Floating windows**), an app opens its window where you last put one of its windows, at the same size, and a new window never opens exactly on top of another: it steps aside. While windows tile, the layout places them.

## Desks

Each screen has 18 *desks* (workspaces), shown nine at a time.

| To… | Do this |
|---|---|
| Bring desk 1–9 (or 10 with <kbd>0</kbd>) to this screen | <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>1</kbd>…<kbd>9</kbd> |
| Send the window to another desk | <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>1</kbd>…<kbd>9</kbd> |
| Go to the next or previous desk | Swipe left or right with three or four fingers |
| Go to the next window | <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>Tab</kbd> or <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>Tab</kbd> (most used first) |
| Go to the next screen | <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>`</kbd> (the key left of <kbd>1</kbd>) |

## The overview

<kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>R</kbd>, or a three-finger swipe up, shows every desk of the screen as a grid of three by three, with their windows. Click a window to go to it; drag a window to another desk to move it there; a digit jumps to that desk; one turn of the wheel shows the next nine desks; <kbd>Esc</kbd> closes. Pressing <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>R</kbd> again tours to the next desk that has windows.

## The spread

A three-finger swipe up on a desk where windows overlap first *spreads* them side by side, so you can pick one. The next swipe up shows the overview. From an app's row in the box, **Show all windows** spreads the windows of that app. On a Mac keyboard, the Mission Control key goes spread → overview → closed.

## Stage

<kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>Return</kbd> (or a four-finger swipe up) puts the window you are working in on a *stage*: alone in the middle, with small pictures of everything else in a row below. Click a picture, or press <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>1</kbd>…<kbd>9</kbd>, to bring it onto the stage. The stage pill on the bar switches between one window and a whole desk. <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>Return</kbd> again puts everything back exactly as it was. If anything goes wrong, <kbd>Super</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Esc</kbd> always leaves the stage.

## Several screens

Plug a screen in and it works. **Controls → Monitors** shows your screens at their real sizes: drag them to arrange them, choose each one's resolution, refresh rate and scale, and whether they **extend**, **mirror** or stay **isolated**. You also choose where the top bar and the dock go (main screen, every screen, or following you), and whether closing the laptop's lid sends everything to the other screen. A screen you plug in again finds its own desks as it left them.
