# Golem

Golem is the one who works behind StandardOS. It keeps your computer up to date, repairs it when something goes wrong, cleans up after itself, and tells you what it did. Golem is not a chat or an assistant you talk to: it is quiet, it works mostly while you are away, and its worth shows when you look.

## What Golem does for you

| Golem's job | When | What you see |
|---|---|---|
| Gets each new version of StandardOS ready | Once a night, in your computer's turn | The new version starts at the next restart; after two days a reminder, *"Golem has an update ready"* |
| Goes back when the desktop fails | When the desktop crashes twice in a row right after starting | A restart on the version before, and a notice saying so |
| Skips a version that broke your desktop | Until a newer one is out | *"Golem went back to the previous version"*; that version is not taken again |
| Keeps your own changes through an update | At each update | *"Golem kept your edits aside"* when both changed the same lines |
| Builds your computer's own system after the install | Once, at the first start | Nothing, if you let it finish; then a restart |
| Keeps the disk tidy | Every day | A notice when the disk is nearly full |
| Starts the desktop again if it closes by itself | Right away | *"The desktop came back on its own"* |

## Golem's laws

Golem follows seven rules. They are not promises: each one is how the system is built.

1. **It works only for you.** Nothing leaves your machines. There is no telemetry, no account, no report sent anywhere.
2. **It does what the configuration says, no more and no less.** Your computer is described by its files; Golem makes the computer match them.
3. **It never breaks what works.** It builds the new version aside, changes over only when that worked, and goes back by itself when it did not.
4. **It does not interrupt.** It waits for a quiet moment — at night, once the desktop has settled — and works at the lowest priority. A new version waits for you to restart.
5. **It does not talk, it tells.** Short notices, in your language, signed Golem.
6. **It never chooses for you.** When something is your decision, it asks once and waits.
7. **You can do more than it can.** Its tools are yours: you can stop it, do what it does yourself, and undo what it did.

## One Golem, many computers

Golem is one and the same in every StandardOS computer: the same name, the same rules, the same way of telling you things. On each of your computers, it works only for you.

## What Golem is not

Golem is not a chatbot and does not read what you do. It does not decide what you should install, watch or write. It does not show you advertising, and it does not need the internet to exist: it lives on your computer and does its work there.

!!! tip "See what Golem did"
    Golem's notices stay in the notification list (the bell in the top bar). For the full detail of an update, open **Controls → Updates**; experts can read the journal — see [Updates in depth](#expert-updates).
