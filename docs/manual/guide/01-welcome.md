# Welcome to StandardOS

StandardOS is an operating system for your computer: the thing that starts when you press the power button, runs your apps, keeps your files and connects you to the internet. It is free, it does not watch you, and it takes care of itself.

This documentation is here for two moments: when you want to learn how something works, and when something is not working the way you expect. You can read it from the start, or search for what you need with the box at the top (press <kbd>/</kbd> to jump there).

## Three things to know

The whole system is built around a simple idea. There are three of you at the computer:

| | What it is | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **You** | The person using the computer | You decide. Everything here works for you. |
| **Standard** | The tools: the system, its apps, and later its hardware | Tools have no will. They do exactly what they are asked. |
| **Golem** | The one who works behind the system | Golem uses the same tools you do: it keeps the computer up to date, repairs it, and tells you what it did. |

The rule that holds it together: **Golem has no power you do not have.** Everything Golem does, it does with the same tools you can use yourself, in plain sight, and you can undo it.

## What makes StandardOS different

**It does not break.** An update is built and tested on its own before it replaces anything. If the new version does not work, your computer keeps the old one. If the desktop ever fails to start, it goes back by itself to the last version that worked.

**It updates itself, quietly.** Golem checks for a new version at night, when you are not using the computer, and gets it ready for the next time you start. You never have to wait for an update in the middle of your work.

**It is yours.** There is no account to create, no advertising and no telemetry: nothing about you or your use of the computer is ever sent anywhere. The system is free software, and every part of it can be read and changed.

**It works on the computer you already have.** StandardOS runs well on machines that are ten years old, and it sets itself up for your hardware when you install it.

**It speaks your language.** The desktop and its apps are available in English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, Italian and German.

## How the screen is organised

The first time you see the desktop, there are four things to recognise:

- **The bar at the top** shows the clock, the open window, the gear (quick settings), the notifications, and small round buttons ("pills") for whatever is happening now. See [The top bar](#guide-top-bar).
- **The dock at the bottom** holds your favourite apps. When a window needs its place it steps aside; touch the bottom edge of the screen with the pointer to bring it back. See [Apps and the dock](#guide-dock).
- **The box** opens with a tap of the <kbd>Super</kbd> key (the key with the Windows or command symbol). Type to find any app, file or setting. See [Apps and the dock](#guide-dock).
- **The desktop** shows the files in your `Desktop` folder, as on any other computer.

## How to use this documentation

The **Guide** (this part) explains StandardOS for everyday use: the screen, settings, apps, files, updates, and what to do when something goes wrong. It needs no technical knowledge.

The **Expert** part explains how the system is made and how to change it beyond the settings: the configuration files, the commands, the update system, recovery and building StandardOS yourself.

!!! tip "This page is on your computer"
    The same documentation is installed on every StandardOS computer and works without internet. Open it from **Controls → Documentation**, or type `manual` in a terminal.
